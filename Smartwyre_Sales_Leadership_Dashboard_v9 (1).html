Territory strategy

Coverage expectations differ by territory shape and sales motion

Brandon Evans

110 accounts • bespoke / high research

Methodical account-by-account work. He has described a cadence of roughly five companies per week with a full work-up before outreach.

Operating focus
  • Use prospecting pools to rank a finite working set
  • Weight strategic fit, potential and buying signal
  • Protect research time; measure account progression, not raw volume

Kiffin Wigert

148 accounts • mid-level distributors

Focused on mid-level distributor accounts. CNI is a representative larger target in the book; the territory strategy still needs further development.

Operating focus
  • Prioritize distributor fit and relationship access
  • Use account priority to identify the next working set
  • Measure progression across named distributor accounts

Jed Cowan

~400 accounts • manufacturer list

Actively reaching out across a roughly 400-account list. Market reactions point to pricing sensitivity among smaller manufacturers.

Operating focus
  • Segment the list by manufacturer profile and price fit
  • Apply Growth Accelerator pricing where appropriate
  • Use prospecting pools to develop a ranked priority list

Donald Timmerman

1,644 accounts • scale / high volume

Large territory, smaller transaction sizes and a high-volume reach-out requirement. Breadth matters more than deep research on every account.

Operating focus
  • Prioritize intent, readiness and recency signals
  • Use scalable sequences and rapid triage
  • Track breadth, response and conversion by account tier

Brett Ingram

108 accounts • expansion-led + selective net new

Early tenure emphasized customer immersion and expansion opportunities, while running selected net-new pursuits in parallel.

Operating focus
  • Move expansion opportunities to forecastable stages
  • Continue selective net-new pursuit: Nutrien, SiteOne, Wilbur-Ellis
  • Use warm introductions and customer context to accelerate access
